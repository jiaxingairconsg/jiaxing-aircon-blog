Aircon Installation in Singapore: Can You Replace Just One Indoor Unit?

You may be able to replace a single indoor aircon unit without replacing the entire multi-split system, but the new unit must be compatible with the existing outdoor condenser. Before arranging aircon installation in Singapore, a technician should verify the model combination, refrigerant, cooling capacity, communication system and condition of existing piping.

How Does a Multi-Split Aircon System Work?

Many Singapore homes use multi-split air conditioning systems, commonly referred to as System 2, System 3 or System 4. These setups connect multiple indoor fan coil units to a shared outdoor condenser, allowing occupants to cool different rooms independently.

Although individual indoor units can be controlled separately, they belong to the same refrigeration system. This means that replacing one component requires more consideration than simply choosing an aircon that is the same size or brand.

When Can You Replace Only One Indoor Aircon Unit?

Partial replacement may be possible when the outdoor condenser is still in good condition and the manufacturer supports the proposed indoor and outdoor model combination. It may also make sense when the other indoor units are functioning normally and the fault is isolated to one room.

However, two units from the same brand are not necessarily compatible. Different product generations may use different refrigerants, controls or communication protocols. Always ask a technician to confirm compatibility using the actual model numbers before purchasing a replacement.

What Should Be Checked Before Replacing an Indoor Unit?

Before recommending an installation, a technician should evaluate several aspects of the existing system:

  • Model compatibility: Confirm the proposed indoor unit is supported by the outdoor condenser.
  • Refrigerant type: Check that both components are designed for the same refrigerant and operating specifications.
  • Cooling capacity: Ensure the combination of indoor units remains within the condenser's supported capacity range.
  • Copper piping and insulation: Assess their condition, dimensions and suitability for the replacement equipment.
  • Drainage and wiring: Verify that the existing layout and connections meet the requirements of the new unit.

The assessment is particularly important in older HDB flats and condominiums, where piping may be concealed behind trunking or built-in carpentry. Our aircon installation checklist explains other considerations before starting installation work.

When Should You Repair, Replace One Unit or Change the Whole System?

Not every aircon breakdown requires replacement. Sometimes, the issue is caused by a faulty component that can be repaired without changing the indoor or outdoor unit.

Can Existing Aircon Piping Be Reused During Installation?

Existing copper piping may sometimes be reused, but only when its condition, size and specifications are suitable for the new equipment and the manufacturer's installation requirements allow it. The technician should also consider whether damaged insulation, contamination or previous leakage affects the suitability of the installation.

Reusing unsuitable piping simply to avoid disruption can create reliability problems. If the replacement requires new pipework, discuss its route, trunking and access before work begins, especially if your home has recently been renovated.

What If You Plan to Replace Other Rooms Later?

Replacing your aircon in stages may be attractive if the remaining units still work. However, confirm that compatible replacement models are available and ask whether future upgrades could require changing the outdoor condenser as well.

It is worth planning around your household's longer-term cooling needs. Review the rooms being cooled, the age of the system and the types of air conditioning systems available in Singapore before deciding whether a partial upgrade is appropriate.
